A parody politics game with an idle business at its heart

A satirical politics game where you build businesses and win elections. Star-Spangled Tycoon is a free parody idle game in your browser.

📱 Free on the App Store▶ Or play in your browser

Star-Spangled Tycoon is a parody politics game built on an idle tycoon. You play a candidate with a long list of cartoon supporters, a campaign budget and a habit of saying the wrong thing at the right time. The core loop is simple. You tap to earn, your businesses earn while you are away, and you spend the money on new projects and upgrades until you are ready for the next election.

The satire is broad on purpose. The characters are a Cabinet of fictional politicians, and the businesses carry joke names. The jokes are aimed at political systems, campaign habits and the absurdity of the process, not at real people or parties. Nothing in the game is meant to represent a real politician, party or government. The full game has 5 worlds and 50 businesses, and each world brings its own angle on the joke.

Parody games sit in an odd spot. They need to be funny and readable, but they also need to play well on their own. Star-Spangled Tycoon tries to do both. The idle structure gives you something to check on each day, while the election mechanic gives you a reason to keep going. Each election resets your progress in exchange for a lasting bonus, so later runs move faster.

The browser version is free to play at the play page, with nothing to download. It is supported by ads at natural breaks, such as after an election or when you collect offline earnings. The iPhone and iPad app is also free on the App Store, with optional purchases inside it. Progress is saved in the browser on your device, and the settings menu can export a save.

If you enjoy satire, or you just want a light game that does not take itself too seriously, this one is worth a session. Open the play page, collect a few dollars, make a campaign decision and see how the joke lands for you. If it does not land, it costs you nothing but a few minutes. The satire is aimed at the process rather than at individuals, so you can enjoy the jokes without following any news story. Each election is a fresh chance to see how far the joke can go before it stops being funny to you. Some jokes are obvious and others take a moment to land, which is part of the fun. The jokes poke at both sides of the aisle rather than favouring one.
